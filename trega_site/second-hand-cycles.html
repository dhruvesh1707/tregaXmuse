Why a second hand cycle is a sensible buy

Many cycles in India are bought with good intentions — for morning exercise, a short office commute, or a child's school run — and then used for a few months before standing in a balcony or parking lot. That makes the used market strong: you can often find a cycle that has done very little riding at well below its shop price. It also means condition varies a lot, because a cycle kept indoors ages very differently from one left in rain and sun.

Your use decides what to buy. A flat 2 km commute, a hilly training ride, and weekend trail riding need three different cycles. Deciding the type first will save you from buying a heavy mountain bike for smooth city roads, or a thin-tyred road cycle for broken streets.

What to check before you buy a cycle

  • Frame size and fit: A cycle that is too large or too small will hurt your knees and back. Match frame size to your height, check seat height adjustment, and confirm you can stand over the frame comfortably.
  • Type — MTB, road or hybrid: MTB for rough tracks, road for smooth tarmac and speed, hybrid for mixed city use. Match the type to your actual roads, not to how the cycle looks.
  • Gears and brakes: Shift through every gear and test both brakes. Gears should move without jumping, the chain should not slip under pressure, and brake pads should still have material left with firm lever feel.
  • Chain wear and rust: A dry, rusty or stretched chain wears out gears quickly. Light surface rust can be cleaned; a loose, sagging chain usually needs replacement, so price that in.
  • Tyres and wheels: Look for cracks, bald patches and perished sidewalls. Spin both wheels — they should run straight without rubbing the brakes. Bent rims are not a quick fix.
  • Geared or single-speed: Single-speed is lighter to maintain for flat areas. Geared suits slopes and longer distances but needs the extra checks above.

What should a second hand cycle cost?

Prices vary by type, brand, frame material, gear set and how much riding the cycle has actually done, so take this only as a rough guide. As a rough guide, basic single-speed city cycles in rideable condition often sell for a couple of thousand rupees, geared hybrids and entry MTBs commonly sit in the mid thousands depending on gear quality and tyre life, and lighter road cycles with better components can go higher, especially if recently serviced with new tyres or brake pads. Keep a small amount aside for a basic service, pump and lock — almost every used cycle needs at least that much attention.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if a cycle frame size fits me?

Frame size should match your height and leg length. As a simple check, you should be able to stand over the top tube with a small gap and reach the handlebar without stretching fully. The listing should state frame size in inches or S, M, L. If it does not, compare the seller's stated rider height in the description with your own height before making an offer.

What is the difference between MTB, road and hybrid cycles?

MTB cycles have wide, knobbly tyres and front suspension for rough ground. Road cycles have thin tyres and a light frame built for speed on smooth roads. Hybrid cycles sit in between, with medium tyres and an upright posture, and suit most city riding and daily commutes best.

Should I choose a geared cycle or a single-speed?

Choose single-speed if your routes are flat and you want simple upkeep. Choose a geared cycle if you face flyovers, slopes or longer rides, as gears make climbing and headwinds much easier. More gears also mean more parts to maintain, so check gear shifting carefully on a geared model.

What should I test on a short ride before buying?

Ride it briefly if you meet the seller: check that gears shift without slipping, both brakes stop firmly without loud squealing, the wheels spin straight, and there is no clicking from the pedals or bottom bracket. Also check tyre grip and whether the seat and handlebar can be adjusted to your height.
