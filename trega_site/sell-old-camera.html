Your old camera is worth more to another photographer

Many cameras sit unused not because they are faulty, but because the owner upgraded, moved to a phone for daily photos, or stopped a hobby. A DSLR or mirrorless body that feels old to you can be the first proper camera for a student, a wedding assistant, or someone starting a small studio in India. Selling it while it still works well, with its battery holding charge and its sensor clean, gets you a better price than waiting until it has gathered dust for two more years.

Buyers for used cameras are careful, and rightly so. They will look closely at your photos, ask about shutter count, and want to know what is included. A clear, honest listing does not lower your price. It does the opposite: it helps the right buyer offer confidently and reduces time wasted on offers from people expecting something different.

How to list it so it sells for a fair price

  • Mention shutter count honestly: Find it from the camera menu or your last service record if you can, and write the actual figure or say clearly if you do not know. Buyers price a low-count body differently from a heavily used one.
  • Clean the sensor and say so: A dusty sensor shows spots on every photo. If you have had it cleaned, mention when. If you have not, do not hide test photos that show the condition.
  • Include the basics: Body cap, battery, charger and strap should be listed and photographed. Missing caps or a third-party charger are not a problem if you state them clearly.
  • Photograph the lens glass in good light: Take close photos of front and rear glass at an angle so scratches, fungus or dust are visible. Also show the mount, screen, dials and any dents on the body.
  • Be clear on kit lens versus body only: State in the title and description whether the price is body only or includes a kit lens, and which lens it is. Most disputes start with this one confusion.

A rough guide to pricing your camera

Prices vary by model, shutter count, sensor condition, age and what is included, so use this only as general guidance. As a rough guide, a body-only camera in clean working order usually fetches less than the same body sold with a kit lens, original battery and charger, and a low shutter count with a clean sensor can support a stronger price than a high-count body that needs cleaning. Recent models and lenses that are still sold new tend to hold value better than older entry models. Set a price you can defend from your photos and details, then let offers show you what buyers nearby are actually willing to pay.

Should I sell my camera body only or with the kit lens?

Both sell, but be explicit. Body only suits buyers who already own lenses, while a kit lens makes the camera ready to use and often attracts first-time buyers. State which option your price covers and photograph the lens glass in good light.

Why does shutter count matter to buyers?

Shutter count gives a rough idea of how much the camera has been used, like kilometres on a vehicle. A lower count suggests less wear. Mention it honestly in your listing, or say clearly if you do not know it.
